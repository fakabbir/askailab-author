Even though Gemini can answer questions using its massive internal knowledge and large context window, you still need Retrieval-Augmented Generation (**RAG**) **to handle private, changing, or massive datasets efficiently**. [[1](https://docs.cloud.google.com/gemini-enterprise-agent-platform/build/rag-engine/rag-overview), [2](https://medium.com/@rapidinnovation/gemini-2-0-flash-is-this-the-end-of-rag-as-we-know-it-dbb05fa80dde), [3](https://medium.com/@muni76590/rag-in-5-minutes-build-a-question-answering-app-with-gemini-your-data-de3ac2496910)]

**Why Gemini Alone Isn't Always Enough**

- **Private and Internal Data:** Gemini does not know your company's private database, internal HR documents, or proprietary codebases unless you feed that data to it. [[1](https://medium.com/@muni76590/rag-in-5-minutes-build-a-question-answering-app-with-gemini-your-data-de3ac2496910), [2](https://docs.cloud.google.com/gemini-enterprise-agent-platform/build/rag-engine/rag-overview)]
- **Cost and Latency:** Passing millions of tokens of raw documents into every single prompt makes API calls very slow and expensive. RAG retrieves only the exact paragraphs you need, saving time and money. [[1](https://medium.com/enterprise-rag/why-gemini-1-5-and-other-large-context-models-are-bullish-for-rag-ce3218930bb4)]
- **Real-Time Updates:** If your internal database changes daily, updating a RAG vector store or using tools like Gemini File Search is much faster than retraining or continuously uploading massive files. [[1](https://www.context-link.ai/blog/rag-for-gemini), [2](https://medium.com/@maliahrajan/the-end-of-rag-how-gemini-file-search-changes-everything-45ddeaeddcfa)]
- **Accuracy and Citations:** RAG forces the model to anchor its answers strictly to the retrieved text chunks, which drastically reduces hallucinations and provides clear source citations.

## **You said: does it matter in terms of speed if there is 1M token or 10M token in context**

does it matter in terms of speed if there is 1M token or 10M token in context

17 September 2026

**Yes, it matters immensely.** The difference in speed between a 1 million (1M) token context and a 10 million (10M) token context is drastic and can turn a near-instant answer into a wait that lasts minutes. [[1](https://medium.com/kairi-ai/the-2m-token-era-what-geminis-ultra-long-context-means-for-every-workflow-6d92a90cd81a)]

To understand why, you have to look at how Large Language Models process data across two distinct phases: **reading your prompt** and **writing the answer**. [[1](https://medium.com/@gezhouz/understanding-llm-response-latency-a-deep-dive-into-input-vs-output-processing-2d83025b8797)]

---

**⏳ Phase 1: Reading (Time to First Token - TTFT)**

When you click send, the model has to "read" your entire input before it can generate its very first word. This initial lag is called **Time to First Token (TTFT)**. [[1](https://support.google.com/gemini/thread/454736631/how-to-prevent-high-latency-and-costs-when-sending-large-context-payloads-pdfs-codebases-to-gemini?hl=en), [2](https://www.kunalganglani.com/blog/llm-latency-benchmark-optimization), [3](https://medium.com/@gezhouz/understanding-llm-response-latency-a-deep-dive-into-input-vs-output-processing-2d83025b8797)]

- **At 1M tokens:** The model is chewing through roughly 750,000 words. Prefilling this context takes several seconds (usually **10 to 30 seconds** depending on the architecture and hardware). [[1](https://redis.io/blog/llm-context-windows/), [2](https://futureagi.com/blog/google-gemini-2-5-pro-2025/)]
- **At 10M tokens:** The mathematical complexity of the model's self-attention mechanism escalates dramatically. The system has to compare millions of tokens against each other. Prefilling a 10M token payload can easily take **2 to 5 minutes** just to begin spitting out an answer. [[1](https://redis.io/blog/llm-context-windows/), [2](https://medium.com/kairi-ai/the-2m-token-era-what-geminis-ultra-long-context-means-for-every-workflow-6d92a90cd81a)]

**✍️ Phase 2: Writing (Tokens Per Second)**

Once the model starts answering, it generates text one token at a time. Every single new word it writes requires it to look back at the entire history in its working memory (called the **KV Cache**). [[1](https://atlan.com/know/llm-context-window-limitations/), [2](https://medium.com/@gezhouz/understanding-llm-response-latency-a-deep-dive-into-input-vs-output-processing-2d83025b8797), [3](https://redis.io/blog/llm-context-windows/)]

- **At 1M tokens:** The KV Cache is large but manageable. The hardware can stream output back to you at a reasonable pace.
- **At 10M tokens:** The KV Cache becomes a massive memory bottleneck. Shuffling that colossal amount of data back and forth inside the GPU's memory slows down the generation speed significantly. A model that normally streams seamlessly will drop to a noticeably slower, sluggish crawl. [[1](https://www.reddit.com/r/LocalLLaMA/comments/1lxuu5m/how_does_having_a_very_long_context_window_impact/), [2](https://atlan.com/know/llm-context-window-limitations/), [3](https://redis.io/blog/llm-context-windows/)]

---

**🛠️ The Feature That Fixes This: Prompt Caching**

Because of these massive latency penalties, modern LLM providers use a feature called **Prompt Caching**. [[1](https://www.kunalganglani.com/blog/llm-latency-benchmark-optimization)]

If you upload 10 million tokens of static data (like a massive library of books or a massive codebase) and ask a question, the first query will take minutes. However, if you keep that same chat window open and ask a follow-up question, the model **caches** (remembers) what it already read. The subsequent responses will feel nearly instantaneous because it skips the multi-minute reading phase. [[1](https://support.google.com/gemini/thread/454736631/how-to-prevent-high-latency-and-costs-when-sending-large-context-payloads-pdfs-codebases-to-gemini?hl=en), [2](https://medium.com/kairi-ai/the-2m-token-era-what-geminis-ultra-long-context-means-for-every-workflow-6d92a90cd81a)]

## **You said: If I have 1000 resumes does it matter if i build RAG or put all the data in the context**

If I have 1000 resumes does it matter if i build RAG or put all the data in the context

17 September 2026

**Yes, it matters immensely**. Even though a 10-million-token context window _can_ technically hold 1,000 resumes, **building a RAG (Retrieval-Augmented Generation) pipeline is vastly superior** to dumping all the data into the context window for this specific use case.

Choosing the right approach depends on three main pillars: **cost**, **speed**, and **accuracy**.

---

**📊 Direct Comparison: RAG vs. Full Context for 1,000 Resumes**

Assuming an average resume is **2 pages (~1,000 words / 1,300 tokens)**, 1,000 resumes equal roughly **1.3 million tokens** of total data.

| **Metric** | **📂 Option A: Put Everything in Context** | **🔍 Option B: Build a RAG Pipeline** |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| **Financial Cost** | 🛑 **Extremely Expensive.** Every single question you ask re-scans 1.3M tokens. A few dozen queries will cost hundreds of dollars. | ✅ **Ultra-Cheap.** You only pay to convert the resumes to numbers once, then pennies per query. |
| **Speed (Latency)** | ⏳ **Slow.** You will wait **15 to 30 seconds** for the model to "read" the context before it starts answering your first question. | ⚡ **Instant.** The system fetches the top 5–10 relevant resumes in milliseconds; the LLM responds instantly. |
| **Accuracy / "Hallucination"** | ⚠️ **Risk of Oversight.** LLMs suffer from "Lost in the Middle"—they struggle to find specific details hidden deep within massive blocks of text. | ✅ **Pinpoint Precision.** The system isolates the exact resumes needed, ensuring the LLM focuses only on the correct candidates. |
| **Setup Effort** | 🛠️ **Zero.** Just drag, drop, and upload the files directly into the prompt box. | ⚙️ **Moderate.** Requires setting up a database to store and retrieve the resumes. |

---

**💸 The Hidden Cost Trap: "Per-Query" Billing**

When you use a long-context window, you pay for the **entire context on every single turn of the conversation**.

If you upload all 1.3M tokens and ask: _"Who has Python experience?"_, you pay for 1.3M tokens. If you then ask a follow-up: _"Great, out of those, who has worked at Google?"_, you pay for another 1.3M tokens. The costs stack up linearly and rapidly, making it financially unsustainable for day-to-day HR operations.

**🎯 When Should You Use Each?**

- **Use RAG if:** You want to build a searchable talent pool, match candidates to job descriptions regularly, filter by specific skills, and keep operating costs to a absolute minimum.
- **Use Full Context if:** You are doing a one-time, highly complex cross-analysis of a small batch of candidates (e.g., comparing the final 5 candidates across 20 different deeply nuanced behavioral metrics where the model needs to hold everything in active memory simultaneously).
