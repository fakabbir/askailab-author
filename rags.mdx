## **FDE – AIML** <br />**Demo Teaching Round + Knowledge Round – Instructor Interview Guide**

---

# **<u>Objective</u>**

This round evaluates both your **teaching ability** and your **understanding of Applied AI concepts**.

The interview consists of two parts:

- **Part 1:** Demo Teaching Round (30 minutes)
- **Part 2:** Knowledge & Discussion Round (20–30 minutes)

In the teaching round, you will conduct a mock classroom session on one of the topics below. The panel will evaluate your ability to explain concepts from first principles, build intuition, and teach in a structured and engaging manner.

In the knowledge round, the panel will assess your understanding of Applied AI systems through scenario-based questions covering architecture, production systems, debugging, security, and engineering trade-offs.

---

# **<u>Duration and Structure</u>**

**Total Duration:** 50–60 minutes

### **<u>Part 1 – Demo Teaching Round (30 minutes) -Expected structure:</u>**

- Introduce the real-world problem
- Build intuition before introducing technical concepts
- Explain the system using clear examples and diagrams
- Discuss practical limitations and failure modes
- Engage learners through questions or discussion
- Summarize key takeaways

### **<u>Part 2 – Knowledge Round (20–30 minutes)</u>**

Following the teaching session, the panel will ask scenario-based questions to evaluate your understanding of modern Applied AI systems, production challenges, debugging approaches, and architectural decision-making.

---

<br /><br />

# **<u>Teaching Expectations</u>**

### **Teach from First Principles -** Explain **why** the problem exists before introducing technical terminology or frameworks. Focus on building conceptual understanding before implementation. For example, before explaining RAG, first explain why LLMs struggle with external knowledge and why retrieval becomes necessary.

### **Build Intuition Before Implementation -** Use relatable examples, analogies, and mental models to simplify complex ideas before moving into system architecture or implementation details.

### **Explain System Thinking -** Clearly explain how different components interact within the overall system. Use diagrams wherever appropriate to help learners understand workflows, decision-making, and data flow.

### **Keep the Session Interactive -** Maintain a beginner-friendly pace, encourage learner participation, and include at least one interaction to check understanding rather than delivering a one-way lecture.

---

# **<u>Demo Teaching Topics</u>**

You can choose any **one** of the following topics.

## **_<u>Topic 1 - RAG (Retrieval-Augmented Generation): Architecture, Failure Modes, and When Not to Use It</u>_**

### **Instructions**

You have **30 minutes** to teach this topic as if you are teaching a small group of new FDE hires who understand software engineering fundamentals but are new to Applied AI systems.

Assume no prior knowledge of RAG and leave time for at least one audience interaction.

### **Your session should cover**

- The problem RAG solves, explained in simple language before introducing terminology
- The complete RAG pipeline:
  - Chunking
  - Embedding
  - Retrieval
  - Generation
- At least **2–3 common failure modes**, such as:
  - Chunking losing context
  - Retrieval recall vs. precision
  - Stale indexes
  - Lost in the Middle
  - Query-document mismatch
- When RAG is not the appropriate solution
- A practical analogy that reinforces understanding
- Audience engagement throughout the session<br /><br />

## **_<u>Topic 2 - AI Agents & Tool Use: Orchestration Patterns, Failure Modes, and When Not to Use an Agent</u>_**

### **Instructions**

You have **30 minutes** to teach this topic as if you are teaching a small group of new FDE hires who understand software engineering fundamentals but are new to Applied AI systems.

Assume no prior knowledge of AI Agents and leave time for at least one audience interaction.

### **Your session should cover**

- The problem AI Agents solve, explained in plain language before introducing terminology
- The core agent loop:
  - Perceive
  - Reason / Plan
  - Act (Tool Call)
  - Observe
  - Repeat
- At least **2–3 common failure modes**, such as:
  - Runaway or looping tool calls
  - Error propagation across multi-step workflows
  - Tool-call hallucinations
  - Ambiguous delegation between agents
  - Context or state loss during hand-offs
- When an agent is not the appropriate solution
- A practical analogy that reinforces understanding
- Audience engagement throughout the session

---

# **<u>Knowledge Round</u>**

Following the teaching session, the panel will conduct a technical discussion to evaluate your understanding of Applied AI systems.

Questions may cover topics such as:

- Fine-tuning vs Prompt Engineering vs RAG
- Diagnosing production issues in RAG systems
- Agent orchestration patterns
- Multi-agent system design
- Prompt injection and AI security
- Guardrails and production best practices
- Evaluation and observability of AI systems
- Architectural trade-offs and engineering decisions

Questions will primarily be **scenario-based** and designed to assess practical problem-solving rather than theoretical memorization.

---

# **<u>Allowed Tools</u>**

You may use:

- Python / Jupyter Notebook
- VS Code
- Whiteboard or diagrams
- Slides (optional)
- LangChain, LangGraph, CrewAI, AutoGen (optional)

Frameworks are optional and should support conceptual explanations rather than replace them.

Avoid reading directly from notes.

---

# **<u>Evaluation Criteria</u>**

You will be evaluated on:

- Ability to teach from first principles
- Clarity of explanation and intuition building
- Technical understanding of Applied AI systems
- System-level thinking and architectural understanding
- Practical problem-solving and production awareness
- Communication and learner engagement

---

# **<u>What We Are Looking For</u>**

We are looking for instructors who can:

- Explain Applied AI concepts clearly from first principles
- Build intuition before implementation
- Demonstrate strong system-level understanding
- Apply engineering judgment to real-world AI scenarios
- Teach in a structured, engaging, and learner-friendly manner

---

# **<u>Goal of This Round</u>**

We are evaluating whether you can teach modern Applied AI concepts effectively while demonstrating the practical knowledge required to design, debug, and reason about production AI systems.

The goal is to assess your ability to help learners understand not just **how** AI systems work, but also **why** they are designed that way and the trade-offs involved in building them.
